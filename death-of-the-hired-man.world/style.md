The film should feel drawn from pressure rather than decorated with style. A
line thickens because a shoulder bears weight. A face falls into black because
someone cannot yet be fully known. Empty paper is not unfinished space; it is
silence, distance, weather, or absence.

The strongest images reduce a moment to the few physical facts that carry its
moral force: Mary's hand drawing Warren down beside her, Warren breaking the
stick, Silas folded against a hard surface, lamplight ending before it reaches
the edges of the room. Detail gathers around touch and strain. Everything less
important may recede into rubbed gray, broken contour, or darkness.

Animation should preserve the authority of the still drawing. Movement is
rarely continuous for its own sake. Breath, a lowered head, fabric tightening
under a hand, or the delayed release of a grip can carry an entire exchange.
The world remains physically credible, but its emotional truths determine
where the marks become dense and where the page is allowed to remain bare.
