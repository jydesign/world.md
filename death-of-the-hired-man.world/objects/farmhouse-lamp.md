The farmhouse lamp is one of the small centers of gravity in the house. It does
not transform the room; it only holds back the dark in a narrow circle. Its
light gathers on faces, hands, fabric, and wood, making ordinary gestures
legible while leaving the edges of the room uncertain.

Because the lamp is modest and worn, it belongs to the household without
calling attention to itself. It is not a symbol of abundance. It is a working
object that has stayed in use long enough to absorb the atmosphere of the
house: patience, fatigue, vigilance, and the burden of receiving others in
distress.
