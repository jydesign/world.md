The stove represents the most practical form of welcome the house can offer:
heat. It asks no moral question and makes no distinction between worker,
relative, guest, and burden. Yet its warmth is limited. It cannot reverse age,
repair pride, or create the reconciliation that has not occurred.

Silas's death beside it turns the farmhouse interior into both refuge and
failure. He has reached warmth and shelter, but not in time to recover, work,
teach, explain, or be consciously forgiven.
