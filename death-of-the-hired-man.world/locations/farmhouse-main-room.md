The main room is where the farm becomes a home, but it is not a room designed
for comfort alone. Eating, waiting, repairing, receiving, and tending all
happen within the same worn enclosure. Its objects are plain because they must
serve repeatedly.

At night, the room breaks into small territories of visibility. The lamp holds
the table. The stove holds the lower part of the room. Beyond them, passages and
corners fall away. Silas occupies this interior while Mary and Warren speak
outside, making the room feel inhabited by a truth neither of them has fully
faced.

Nothing in the room announces death. That is what makes the death severe. Tea,
chairs, heat, and household work remain ordinary around it.
